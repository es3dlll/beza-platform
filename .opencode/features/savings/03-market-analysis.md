# Savings Market Analysis

## Syrian Savings Landscape

### Current State
- **Bank penetration**: < 20% of adults have a bank account
- **Formal savings rate**: < 5% of GDP (vs 20%+ in MENA peers)
- **Common savings methods**: Cash under mattress, gold/jewelry, real estate, informal jameyaat
- **Trust in banks**: Extremely low (historical freezes, capital controls, sanctions)
- **Islamic finance demand**: > 80% of Syrians prefer Sharia-compliant products

### Jameyaat (Informal Rotating Savings)
- Estimated 60%+ of households participate in at least one jameya
- Typical group size: 5–20 people
- Monthly contribution: 10K–500K SYP per person
- Key risks: Default by member, no legal recourse, cash handling, no interest earned
- Beza Savings Team Goals are a direct digital replacement

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Type | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|------|-----------|------------|
| Traditional banks (Bemo, BSF, Syria Int'l) | Regulated savings accounts | Government backing | Interest-based (riba), low trust, branch-only |
| Mobile wallets (Syriatel Cash, MTN Cash) | Mobile money | Large agent network | No dedicated savings features, no goal tracking |
| Gold | Physical asset | Inflation hedge, liquid | Storage risk, divisible, no yield |
| Real estate | Physical asset | Value store, rent income | Illiquid, high entry barrier |
| Informal jameyaat | Social | Trust-based, flexible | Default risk, undocumented |

### Market Opportunity
- **Total addressable market**: 8M Syrian adults with mobile phones
- **Serviceable addressable market**: 3M with smartphones + Beza wallet
- **Serviceable obtainable market (Y3)**: 500K savings users
- **Average savings potential per user**: 50K–500K SYP per goal

### Key Trends
1. Rising digital adoption post-COVID: USSD → app migration accelerating
2. Inflation drives need for quick goal achievement (SYP depreciates ~50%/year)
3. Young demographic (65% under 30) open to digital financial tools
4. Religious observance drives demand for Sharia-compliant alternatives
5. Diaspora remittances ($2B+/year) create funding sources for savings
